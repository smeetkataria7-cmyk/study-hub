21

Frequency graphs & ogives

Draw the distribution, then read it.
21.1

Frequency distribution formulas

relative frequency  f / N   (percent  f/N × 100)frequency density  fclass width

Use frequency density as the bar height when class widths are unequal. Class width, mid-value, Sturges' rule and boundary conversion are in section 2.

21.2

Cumulative frequency

  • Less than — running total from the first class; plot against upper boundaries.
  • More than — running total from the last class; plot against lower boundaries.
21.3

Graph types for a frequency table

  • Histogram — adjacent bars on class boundaries.
  • Frequency polygon — join (mid-value, f) points, closed with zero-frequency classes at both ends.
  • Frequency curve — smooth freehand curve through the polygon points.
  • Ogive — cumulative frequency curve; median read off where the two ogives meet.
Fig. 5

Less-than and more-than ogives meet at the median

The less-than curve is plotted at upper boundaries and rises; the more-than curve is plotted at lower boundaries and falls. They cross where the cumulative frequency is N/2, whose x-value is the median.

22.1

Quantile plot

fi = i − 0.5n

Sort the data x1 ≤ … ≤ xn and plot xi against fi: about 100 fi% of the data lies at or below xi. Shows the whole distribution, quartiles and outliers.

22.2

Quantile–quantile (Q–Q) plot

Plot the quantiles of one distribution against the same quantiles of another (or of a normal distribution). Points on the 45° line mean the same distribution; a shift or bend shows a difference in location, spread or skew.

22.3

Outlier detection

  • Boxplot fences: Q1 − 1.5,IQR and Q3 + 1.5,IQR.
  • z-score: |z| > 3.
  • Isolated points in a scatter plot.
22.4

Scatter plot and pair-plot

A scatter plot shows correlation, clusters and outliers for two attributes; a pair-plot is the grid of all pairs.

  • Limits: only pairwise views, overplotting for large n, n(n − 1)/2 plots for n attributes, cannot show higher-order relations.
22.5

Complex data and relations

Tag clouds for text, network/graph diagrams for relationships, heat maps for matrices, treemaps for hierarchies.

22.6

Scoreboard vs dashboard

  • Scoreboard — tracks performance against targets and KPIs over time (strategic, periodic).
  • Dashboard — monitors current operations in near real time (operational, at a glance).
22.7

Graph vs chart

A graph shows the mathematical relation between variables on x–y axes (line, scatter). A chart is any visual display of data (pie, bar, table, map). Every graph is a chart; not every chart is a graph.

22.8

Tools

  • Weka — Java tool with Explorer (Preprocess, Classify, Cluster, Associate, Select attributes, Visualize), Experimenter and KnowledgeFlow; reads ARFF and CSV.
  • RapidMiner — drag-and-drop operators joined into a process; repository, design and results views.

5

Visualization

Pictures that reveal structure, and the tools to make them.
5.1

Why visualize data?

People grasp patterns, trends and outliers in pictures far faster than in tables. Visualization supports exploration, explanation and decision making.

  • Good graphics are accurate, clear, simple (no chart junk), labelled and honest (axes not truncated to mislead).
  • Pick the chart for the question: comparison, distribution, relationship, composition or change over time.

5.4

Quantile and Q–Q plots

  • Quantile plot: sorted values plotted against their cumulative proportion (i − 0.5)/n; shows the whole distribution, median and quartiles.
  • Q–Q plot: quantiles of one distribution against another (or a normal). Points near the 45° line = same distribution; a parallel shift = different location; a different slope = different spread; curved ends = skew or heavy tails. Used to check normality.
5.5

Scatter plots and pair-plots

  • Each point is one object plotted on two attributes; shows correlation, clusters, outliers and non-linear patterns.
  • A pair-plot (scatter-plot matrix) shows every pair of attributes; colour by class to see separation.
  • Limits: only two variables at a time, overplotting with many points, n(n − 1)/2 plots for n attributes, cannot show higher-order relations.
5.6

Detecting outliers visually

  • Boxplot points beyond the whiskers; histogram isolated bars; scatter-plot points far from the cloud; Q–Q plot points off the line.
  • Check whether an outlier is an error (fix or remove) or a genuine rare event (keep, investigate).

5.8

Visualizing complex data and relations

  • Tag clouds — word size shows frequency (text data).
  • Network / graph diagrams — nodes and links (social networks).
  • Heat maps — colour-coded matrix (correlations, gene expression).
  • Treemaps — nested rectangles for hierarchies and proportions.
5.9

Scoreboard vs dashboard; graph vs chart

BasisScoreboardDashboard
PurposeTracks performance against targets and KPIsMonitors current operations
Time framePeriodic (strategic)Near real time (operational)
AudienceManagementOperators and analysts

A graph shows the mathematical relation between variables on axes (line, scatter); a chart is any visual display (pie, bar, table, map). Every graph is a chart, not every chart is a graph. Dashboard rules: show the key measures on one screen, use simple charts, consistent colours, and highlight exceptions.

5.10

Tools: Weka and RapidMiner

  • Weka: open-source Java toolkit from the University of Waikato. Explorer tabs: Preprocess, Classify, Cluster, Associate, Select attributes, Visualize; also Experimenter and KnowledgeFlow. Reads ARFF and CSV.
  • RapidMiner: drag-and-drop workflow tool; chain operators (read data, preprocess, model, evaluate) into a process; repository, design and results views.
  • Both support cleaning, normalisation, classification, clustering and visualisation without heavy programming; Tableau and Power BI are common for dashboards.

1. Evolution of FinTech in India

FinTech (financial technology) is the use of software, internet and mobile technology to deliver financial services: payments, lending, investing, insurance and banking. India has one of the three largest FinTech ecosystems in the world and the highest FinTech adoption rate among major economies. The growth came from three forces together: government-built digital public infrastructure, cheap smartphones and mobile data, and supportive regulation.

3. How FinTech solves problems that banks faced

FinTech has moved from being seen as a competitor to banks to being their main innovation partner. Banks bring licences and money; FinTechs bring technology, distribution and speed.

GoalProblem faced by banksFinTech solution
Financial inclusionFew physical branches in rural India; branches are costly to runMobile banking, UPI, AePS and banking correspondents let rural users check balances, send money and receive government benefits (DBT) on their phones
Mutual funds, insurance and shares were hard to access outside metrosApps like Groww and Zerodha allow investing from ₹100 with paperless KYC
Bridging the credit gapBanks want paperwork, collateral and a credit history; thin-file customers get rejectedLenders use alternative data (UPI history, GST returns, bill payments) to give instant small loans to street vendors and small businesses
MSMEs face cash-flow gaps and long loan approval timesInvoice financing and quick working-capital loans approved in hours
Lower transaction costsBank transfers had charges and delaysUPI makes person-to-person and merchant payments free and instant
Physical KYC made onboarding expensivee-KYC and video KYC onboard customers in minutes at a fraction of the cost
Digitising the informal economyCash leaves no record, so small traders cannot prove incomeQR-code payments create a digital transaction history that lenders can use
Cash economy means lower tax collection and counterfeit notesDigital payments improve traceability, reduce printing costs and help tax collection

4. India Stack

India Stack is a set of open APIs and digital public goods built by the government. Any bank or startup can use them, which is the main reason Indian FinTech scaled so cheaply.

LayerWhat it doesComponents
Presence-lessProve identity without being physically presentAadhaar biometric and OTP authentication
PaperlessSign up and sign documents with no paperAadhaar e-KYC, DigiLocker, e-Sign
CashlessMove money digitally between any banksUPI, IMPS, AePS, Aadhaar Payments Bridge
Consent layerShare financial data safely, only with the user's permissionAccount Aggregator framework, DEPA

5. Key drivers of FinTech growth in India

  • Digital public infrastructure: India Stack (above) gives every FinTech free or cheap identity, payment and data rails.
  • Venture capital: billions of dollars of investment turned startups such as Paytm, PhonePe, Razorpay, Zerodha and CRED into large companies (unicorns).
  • Demographic dividend: median age about 28; a young, smartphone-first population prefers apps to bank branches.
  • Internet penetration: over a billion mobile connections and some of the world's cheapest mobile data.
  • Government push: JAM trinity (Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar, Mobile), Digital India, demonetisation, DBT.
  • AI-powered underwriting: credit scoring from GST filings, UPI history and bill payments for people with no credit history.
  • Small-ticket loans: FinTech NBFCs handle most small personal loans, important for young borrowers.
  • Democratisation of investing and insurance: discount brokers, SIPs from ₹100, robo-advisory and micro-insurance.
  • Supportive regulation: RBI regulatory sandbox, Account Aggregator licences, payment aggregator guidelines.
  • COVID-19: forced contactless payments and remote onboarding.

8. Role of tech companies in finance

Tech companies play three roles. They usually do not hold the customer's money; a licensed bank or NBFC does.

RoleWhat they doExamples

9. Cryptocurrency challenges in India

Crypto is legal to hold and trade in India but is not legal tender. It is classed as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) and is tightly controlled.

ChallengeDetails
Heavy taxationFlat 30% tax on profits; no set-off of losses (a loss on Ethereum cannot reduce tax on a Bitcoin gain); 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000, which locks up traders' money
RBI opposition and no legal-tender statusRBI warns crypto threatens monetary sovereignty and financial stability and prefers its own e-Rupee (CBDC). No regulator protects investors if an exchange collapses or a fraud happens
AML complianceCrypto exchanges, including foreign ones, must register with FIU-IND as reporting entities under the PMLA; unregistered exchanges get blocked
Reporting and trackingIncome-tax rules require detailed reporting; the government cross-checks with data from exchanges; heavy penalties for mistakes; traders move to foreign platforms (capital flight)
Other risksHigh volatility, scams and fake coins, hacking of exchanges, regulatory uncertainty

10. Case study: how Policybazaar disrupted insurance

Policybazaar (PB Fintech, founded 2008) turned an agent-driven "push" market, where agents sold whatever paid the most commission, into a transparent, customer-driven "pull" marketplace.

  1. Comparison marketplace: policies from dozens of insurers on one screen; customers compare and choose for themselves.
  2. Price transparency: premiums, riders, exclusions and Claim Settlement Ratios shown openly; competition lowered premiums.
  3. Promoted term and health insurance: agents pushed high-commission ULIPs and endowment plans; Policybazaar marketed cheap, high-cover term plans.
  4. Claims support: dedicated claims assistance, hospital and garage locators, the "PB Care" service.
  5. Asset-light model: it does not carry insurance risk; it earns commission and grows mainly through online traffic, at low cost.

11. Case study: CRED

Business model: CRED is a members-only app for people with a credit score of 750+. Paying credit-card bills is free; this attracts a wealthy, low-risk user base, which CRED earns from by:

  • Brand partnerships: premium brands pay to list offers and products in the CRED Store and rewards.
  • Lending: CRED Cash (personal loans), CRED Mint (P2P), earning interest and referral fees.
  • Transaction fees: about 1–1.5% on RentPay and similar card payments.
  • Insurance and wealth referrals.

Why customer acquisition worked: the 750+ gate made it feel like an exclusive club; it rewarded something users already did (paying card bills) with coins and cashback; viral, retro-celebrity ads made it a talking point; heavy early cash-burn on rewards built a valuable audience before monetising.

13. Stripe and Square

Stripe (founded 2010 by Patrick and John Collison) is an online payments and financial infrastructure company known for developer-friendly APIs. Services: online payment processing, payment gateway, Stripe Billing (subscriptions and invoices), international payments in many currencies, Stripe Radar (ML fraud detection), Stripe Terminal, Stripe Connect (marketplaces) and Stripe Capital (business loans).

Square (now Block, founded 2009 by Jack Dorsey and Jim McKelvey) started with a small card reader that plugged into a phone. Services: POS systems, card readers and terminals, online store and payment links, business software (inventory, staff, sales reports), invoices, payroll and Square Loans. It also owns Cash App.

Practice questions

Name the four phases of FinTech evolution in India with one milestone each.

Foundation (pre-2000: CBS, NEFT/RTGS), Growth (2000–15: Aadhaar e-KYC, IMPS, PMJDY), Acceleration (2016–20: UPI, demonetisation), Current (2020+: Account Aggregator, embedded finance, credit on UPI).

Which regulator oversees each: a neo-bank, a robo-advisor, an online insurance marketplace?

Neo-bank: RBI (through the partner bank). Robo-advisor: SEBI. Insurance marketplace: IRDAI.

What are the four layers of India Stack?

Presence-less (Aadhaar authentication), paperless (e-KYC, DigiLocker, e-Sign), cashless (UPI, AePS, IMPS), consent layer (Account Aggregator).

What is MDR?

Merchant Discount Rate: the percentage fee a payment gateway or bank charges a merchant on each card or wallet transaction, typically 1–3%. UPI person-to-merchant payments have zero MDR.

How does a FinTech lender give credit to someone with no credit history?

By using alternative data: UPI and bank transaction history, GST returns, utility bill payments, e-commerce sales, device data, analysed by ML models.

Why can't Fi or Jupiter open bank accounts on their own?

India does not issue standalone digital-only banking licences, so neo-banks partner with licensed banks (Federal Bank, Equitas etc.), which hold the deposits.

State three tax rules for crypto in India.

30% flat tax on gains; losses cannot be set off against other gains; 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000.

Explain the 'push to pull' shift Policybazaar created.

Earlier, agents pushed products that earned them the highest commission. Policybazaar let customers pull information themselves by comparing policies, prices and claim ratios, then choose.

Why is CRED's 750+ credit-score rule good for its business?

It filters for affluent, low-risk users, making the audience valuable to premium brands and lenders, and it creates an 'exclusive club' image that drives sign-ups.

Give two reasons an e-commerce site uses Razorpay rather than a free UPI QR.

Automatic order confirmation through API integration, and support for cards, net banking, EMI and subscriptions (also smart routing, refunds dashboard).

Stripe vs Square in one line each.

Stripe: API-first online payments infrastructure for internet businesses. Square: hardware-plus-software POS system for small physical businesses.

What is embedded finance? Give an example.

Offering a financial product inside a non-financial platform at the point of need, e.g. Amazon Pay Later at checkout, or Zomato offering insurance on deliveries.

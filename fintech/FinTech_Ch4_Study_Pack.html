1. Online banking

Definition: online banking (internet banking or net banking) is a digital banking service that lets customers access their accounts and carry out transactions through the bank's website, without visiting a branch.

Importance in digital financial services:

  • Convenience: bank from anywhere with an internet connection.
  • 24×7 availability, including holidays.
  • Fast fund transfers through NEFT, RTGS and IMPS.
  • Bill payments for electricity, phone, water and other utilities.
  • Account management: balance, statements, transaction history.
  • Time and cost saving for customers and reduced branch workload for banks.
  • Security through passwords, OTPs and multi-factor authentication.
  • Financial inclusion: banking for people far from a branch.

Services offered: balance enquiry and account details; fund transfers (own accounts, other banks); bill payments; online shopping payments; cheque book requests and stop-payment; opening FDs and RDs; loan details, EMI payment and loan applications; downloadable statements; SMS and email alerts.

2. Mobile banking: architecture and working

Mobile banking brings banking services to native smartphone apps. It uses a multi-tier client–server architecture built on microservices, a secure API gateway and central data governance.

Working (one transaction, step by step):

  1. User opens the app and logs in with fingerprint, face or MPIN.
  2. App encrypts the request (e.g. 'transfer ₹500') and sends it over HTTPS/TLS.
  3. API gateway and IAM validate the token, check authorisation and apply rate limits.
  4. Gateway routes the request to the payments microservice, which talks to the core banking system.
  5. Core banking debits and credits the ledgers; results are saved in the data layer and logged for audit.
  6. Notification service sends an SMS or push alert; the app shows a confirmation screen.

4. Digital wallets

Definition: a digital wallet (e-wallet) is a software application that securely stores payment credentials (cards, bank links) and/or pre-loaded money, and lets the user pay quickly online and offline.

Types: closed wallets (usable only with one merchant, e.g. Amazon Pay balance for Amazon), semi-closed (usable with partner merchants, e.g. Paytm wallet), open (can also withdraw cash; issued by banks). In India these are called Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs) and are regulated by the RBI.

Features: stored value or linked accounts; one-touch payments by QR, NFC or one-click checkout; tokenisation of card numbers; loyalty cards, coupons, tickets and boarding passes in one place; transaction history.

Applications: e-commerce checkout; P2P transfers; in-store QR payments at kirana shops; transport fares, FASTag tolls and bill payments; mobile recharges.

5. How a digital wallet transaction works

User ──scan QR / tap NFC──▶ Wallet app ──biometric/PIN──▶ token created
   ──encrypted token──▶ Payment gateway ──▶ Card network / NPCI switch
   ──▶ Issuing bank (verify, check balance, debit) ──▶ Merchant's acquiring bank (credit)
   ──auth code──▶ Wallet app: receipt + SMS/push to both parties
  1. Initiation: customer scans a QR, taps an NFC reader or selects the wallet at online checkout.
  2. Authentication: fingerprint, face ID or PIN on the phone.
  3. Tokenisation and encryption: card details are replaced by a one-time token and sent encrypted.
  4. Routing: the payment gateway sends it to the acquiring bank and the network switch (Visa, Mastercard, NPCI).
  5. Verification and debit: the issuing bank validates the token, checks the balance, debits the customer and authorises credit to the merchant.
  6. Confirmation: an authorisation code comes back; receipt and alerts are sent.

6. Mobile payment systems

Definition: the complete ecosystem of apps, payment networks, banks, clearing systems and regulations that lets people and businesses start, approve, process and settle payments with a mobile phone. Examples: UPI, mobile wallets, NFC tap-to-pay, mobile POS.

Role in digital financial services:

  • Cashless economy: every phone becomes a payment terminal.
  • Wider merchant acceptance: a printed QR code costs nothing compared with a card machine.
  • Faster money movement: instant settlement improves working capital.
  • Credit footprints: transaction history helps unbanked small traders get loans.
  • Direct Benefit Transfer: subsidies paid straight into citizens' accounts, cutting leakage and middlemen.

8. APIs and their role in financial services

Definition: an Application Programming Interface (API) is a set of rules and protocols that lets one software application request data or services from another in a standard, secure way. Think of it as a waiter: the app (customer) places an order, the API (waiter) carries it to the bank's system (kitchen) and brings back the result.

Open banking means banks expose selected services through secure APIs so that authorised third parties (FinTechs) can build products on top, with the customer's consent.

  • Enable open banking innovation without exposing the bank's core database.
  • Decouple architecture: the old core banking system stays behind; new apps can be built quickly in front.
  • Remove friction: instant KYC, credit checks and balance checks without documents or branch visits.
  • Interoperability: connect payment networks, e-commerce sites, credit bureaus and insurers.

FinTech app ──(1) REST request, JSON, OAuth token──▶ Bank API Gateway
                                                     │ (2) validate API key, signature,
                                                     │     OAuth 2.0 token, rate limit
                                                     ▼
                                     (3) translate to internal commands
                                                     ▼
                                        Core Banking System (CBS)
                                     (4) read/update ledger, return result
                                                     ▼
FinTech app ◀──(5) encrypted JSON response────── API Gateway
  1. Request: the app (e.g. a trading app checking a balance) sends a REST call in JSON on behalf of the logged-in user.
  2. Security validation: the bank's gateway checks the API key, digital signature, OAuth 2.0 token and rate limits.
  3. Translation and routing: the gateway converts the request into internal commands for the core banking system.
  4. Execution: the CBS reads or updates its ledgers and returns the result.
  5. Response: the gateway sends back an encrypted JSON response and the app updates its screen.

11. Peer-to-peer (P2P) payment systems

Definition: platforms and networks that let one individual send money directly to another from a bank account or wallet, without cash, cheques or branch visits. Examples: UPI apps, PayPal, Venmo, Zelle.

  1. Onboarding: register and link a bank account, card or create a VPA.
  2. Choose recipient: by phone number, email, VPA (name@bank) or QR code.
  3. Authorise: enter amount and approve with UPI PIN or biometrics.
  4. Clearing: the app sends the instruction to a central switch (NPCI for UPI).
  5. Settlement and alert: sender debited, receiver credited, both notified in real time.

12. PayPal

PayPal is a global online payments company (founded 1998) for P2P transfers and online shopping across more than 200 countries.

Features: cross-border payments with automatic currency conversion; buyer and seller protection with dispute resolution; pay using just an email address, without sharing bank details; multiple funding sources (PayPal balance, cards, bank account).

Working: (1) sender enters recipient's email or phone and the amount; (2) PayPal draws funds from the sender's balance or linked card or bank; (3) PayPal updates its internal ledger, debiting the sender and crediting the recipient's PayPal account; (4) recipient is notified and can keep the balance or withdraw to a bank account.

Key point: PayPal holds money in its own wallet ledger. Google Pay in India does not; it moves money directly between banks through UPI.

13. Google Pay and UPI

Features: direct bank-to-bank settlement through UPI (no wallet balance); payments using a VPA (e.g. name@okaxis) or phone contacts, no account number or IFSC needed; QR and NFC payments in shops; security through device lock, UPI PIN, tokenisation and AI fraud checks; bill payments and recharges.

Payer (Google Pay = PSP app) ──encrypted UPI request──▶ NPCI UPI switch
          ◀── UPI PIN entry (validated by payer's bank) ──
NPCI ──debit instruction──▶ Payer's bank
NPCI ──credit instruction──▶ Payee's bank
Both parties ◀── push notification, SMS, receipt
  1. User picks a contact or scans a QR and enters the amount.
  2. Google Pay, acting as a Payment Service Provider (PSP) app, sends an encrypted UPI request to the NPCI switch.
  3. User enters the UPI PIN on a secure screen; the payer's bank verifies it.
  4. NPCI tells the payer's bank to debit and the payee's bank to credit, in real time.
  5. Both get alerts and an in-app receipt.

Practice questions

Differentiate NEFT, RTGS and IMPS.

NEFT settles in half-hourly batches, any amount; RTGS settles each transaction in real time, minimum ₹2 lakh; IMPS is instant, 24×7, for smaller urgent transfers.

Name the layers of a mobile banking architecture.

Client tier (apps), API gateway with identity and access management, backend microservices, data layer, plus cross-cutting security, compliance and monitoring.

What does USSD *99# allow?

Basic banking (balance, transfers) on feature phones without internet.

What is tokenisation in a wallet?

Replacing the real card number with a random one-time token for each transaction, so the real number is never exposed to the merchant.

What is a PPI?

Prepaid Payment Instrument: RBI's term for wallets and prepaid cards that hold stored value; types are closed, semi-closed and open.

Why does a small merchant prefer a UPI QR code over a card machine?

It is free (zero MDR on UPI), needs no hardware and settles instantly.

Can a digital wallet be used without a bank account?

Yes, a stored-value (minimum-KYC) wallet can be loaded with cash or another method, within RBI limits.

What is open banking?

Banks sharing selected data and services with authorised third parties through secure APIs, with the customer's consent.

List the five steps of an API call to a bank.

Request from the app; validation at the gateway (keys, OAuth token, rate limit); translation and routing to the CBS; ledger execution; encrypted JSON response.

What role does NPCI play in a Google Pay payment?

It runs the UPI switch that routes the request, and instructs the payer's bank to debit and the payee's bank to credit.

PayPal vs Google Pay (India): one key difference.

PayPal stores money in its own wallet ledger and settles internally; Google Pay moves money directly between bank accounts over UPI.

What is a VPA?

Virtual Payment Address, such as name@okaxis: a UPI ID that hides the bank account number and IFSC.

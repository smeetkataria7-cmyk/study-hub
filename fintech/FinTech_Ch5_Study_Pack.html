1. Cybersecurity challenges in digital finance

Cybersecurity means protecting financial systems, networks and data from unauthorised access, damage or theft. Digital finance is convenient but gives criminals more ways to attack.

ChallengeWhat happensExample
Phishing and social engineeringFake messages, websites or calls trick users into revealing passwords, PINs or OTPsSMS: 'Your account will be blocked, click here' leading to a fake bank site
Malware and ransomwareHarmful software steals data or locks systems for ransomFake banking app that records keystrokes
Identity and credential theftStolen passwords, card details or ID used to take over accountsSIM-swap fraud to receive the victim's OTPs
Data breachesWeak security exposes customer dataLeak of card or KYC data from a company's database

2. Financial fraud

Financial fraud is the intentional use of deception to steal money, gain a financial benefit or misuse someone's financial information. Fraudsters exploit trust, urgency and lack of awareness.

TypeHow it worksExample
Phishing / vishing / smishingFake email, call or SMS collects credentialsEmail copying the bank's logo asking you to 'update KYC'
UPI and QR-code fraudVictim is told to scan a QR or approve a 'collect' request to receive money, but actually paysBuyer on OLX sends a QR 'to pay you' for your sofa
Card fraudStolen or skimmed card details used for purchasesSkimmer fitted on an ATM
Impersonation fraudPretending to be a bank officer, police, courier or customer care'Customer care' asks for an OTP to 'reverse' a payment
Loan app fraudIllegal apps give small loans at huge charges and harass borrowersApp demands access to contacts and threatens family
Investment / Ponzi scamsPromises of guaranteed high returnsFake crypto or trading tip groups

3. Preventing fraud

  • Never share PIN, password, CVV or OTP with anyone, even someone claiming to be from the bank.
  • Use only official apps and websites; check the URL before logging in.
  • Check the receiver's name and amount before confirming.
  • You never need to enter a UPI PIN to receive money.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Turn on transaction alerts and review statements regularly.
  • Avoid banking on public Wi-Fi; keep phone, browser and apps updated.
  • Report suspicious transactions immediately to the bank and on the national cybercrime helpline (1930) or cybercrime.gov.in.
  • For banks and platforms: fraud monitoring, device binding, transaction limits for new payees, cooling periods, customer awareness campaigns.

4. Data privacy vs data security

Banks hold names, account numbers, transaction histories, ID documents and credit records. Privacy is about collecting and using this data fairly; security is about protecting it.

BasisData privacyData security
ConcernWhether data is collected and used fairly, for a clear purposeHow data is protected from theft, loss or unauthorised access
Key question'Should we collect or use this data?''How do we keep this data safe?'
MethodsConsent, purpose limitation, data minimisation, clear notices, user rightsEncryption, access control, backups, authentication, monitoring
Governed byLaws such as India's DPDP Act 2023, GDPRStandards such as PCI DSS, ISO 27001, RBI cyber-security framework
ExampleCollect only the details needed for a loan applicationEncrypt applicants' documents and restrict staff access

Both are needed: a system can be secure yet misuse data, or have a good privacy policy yet weak protection.

6. Multi-factor authentication (MFA)

MFA verifies a user with two or more different types of evidence:

FactorMeaningExamples
KnowledgeSomething you knowPassword, PIN, security question
PossessionSomething you havePhone receiving an OTP, authenticator app, hardware token, debit card
InherenceSomething you areFingerprint, face, iris

How it works: enter username and password → system asks for a second factor (OTP, app approval, biometric) → access only after both checks pass.

Why it improves security: a stolen password alone is not enough to log in; account takeover becomes much harder. Users must still never share OTPs or approve unexpected login requests. Example: UPI uses the registered phone (possession) plus the UPI PIN (knowledge).

10. Regulatory compliance

Regulatory compliance means following all the laws, rules, directions and standards that apply to a financial service. Which rules apply depends on the product, the customers and the data handled.

  • Customer protection: fair treatment and clear information.
  • Prevention of financial crime: KYC and AML controls.
  • Data protection: responsible handling of personal data (DPDP Act).
  • Trust and stability: reduces legal and operational risk; builds confidence.
  • Accountability: records, audits and complaint handling show the rules are followed.
  • Avoiding penalties: non-compliance can bring fines, licence cancellation or bans (e.g. RBI action against Paytm Payments Bank in 2024).

Indian regulators: RBI (banks, payments, NBFCs, digital lending), SEBI (securities, brokers, investment advisers), IRDAI (insurance), PFRDA (pensions), FIU-IND (anti-money-laundering reporting), MeitY / Data Protection Board (personal data).

11. Role of the RBI

  • Regulation and supervision of banks, NBFCs and other entities under its remit.
  • Payment-system oversight under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007: authorises payment operators like NPCI, wallets and payment aggregators.
  • Customer protection: limited liability for unauthorised digital transactions, grievance redressal, the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme.
  • Risk management: cyber-security and IT risk directions for regulated entities.
  • Financial integrity: KYC Master Direction and AML requirements.
  • Enabling innovation: regulatory sandbox, Account Aggregator framework, e-Rupee (CBDC) pilot.
  • Digital Lending Guidelines (2022): direct disbursal to borrower, Key Fact Statement, curbs on data access.

12. KYC and AML

KYC (Know Your Customer) is verifying a customer's identity and details before and during the relationship. Forms: physical KYC, Aadhaar e-KYC, video KYC (V-CIP), Central KYC (CKYC) registry.

AML (Anti-Money Laundering) refers to measures that stop criminals from making illegal money look legitimate. Money laundering has three stages: placement (putting cash into the system), layering (moving it around to hide its origin) and integration (bringing it back as apparently clean money). In India AML rests on the PMLA 2002; suspicious transactions are reported to FIU-IND.

  • Identity verification stops accounts in fake names.
  • Risk categorisation decides which customers need closer checks.
  • Transaction monitoring spots unusual patterns (many small deposits, money quickly moved out: 'mule accounts').
  • Record keeping and reporting (STRs, CTRs) help investigators.
  • Together they protect customers and the integrity of the financial system.

Assign clear owners (compliance officer), train employees, hold vendors to the same standards, and treat compliance as a continuous cycle, not a one-time checklist. Example: before launching a new payment feature, assess risks, test it, explain it clearly to customers and set up complaint and incident handling.

14. RBI regulatory sandbox

A regulatory sandbox is a controlled live-testing environment where new FinTech products are tried with real but limited customers, under the regulator's supervision and with some relaxed rules, before full launch. RBI issued its sandbox framework in 2019 and runs it in themed cohorts (e.g. retail payments, cross-border payments, MSME lending, fraud prevention).

Application ─▶ Preliminary screening ─▶ Test design ─▶ Application assessment
                                                              │
           Exit / full launch ◀── Evaluation ◀── Testing (limited users, time-bound)
  • Customer protection: risks found before reaching millions of users.
  • Controlled testing under defined limits (users, amount, time).
  • Encourages innovation: startups can test without full licensing burden.
  • Risk assessment: security, operational and compliance weaknesses identified.
  • Better regulation: the regulator learns about new technology and can write better rules.
  • Improvement before launch from real feedback.

Example: an AI-based lending app is tested with a few thousand users for six months; defaults, fairness and complaints are studied before a wider launch.

16. Ethical issues in FinTech

  • Privacy: collecting more data than needed or using it for unrelated purposes.
  • Bias and discrimination: credit or insurance algorithms may unfairly disadvantage groups (by gender, area, caste proxies) if data or models are biased.
  • Lack of transparency: hidden fees; 'black-box' decisions customers cannot understand.
  • Manipulative design (dark patterns): nudging users to borrow, invest or share data.
  • Predatory lending and over-indebtedness: easy instant loans at high cost.
  • Accountability: someone must own errors, complaints and security failures.
  • Digital divide: people without smartphones or digital skills get left out.

17. Responsible use of AI

AI is used for fraud detection, chatbots, credit scoring and monitoring. Responsible AI principles:

PrincipleMeaning
FairnessTest that decisions do not unjustifiably differ between groups
Transparency / explainabilityExplain decisions in plain language, especially rejections
Privacy and securityUse data lawfully and protect it
Accuracy and monitoringTest before use; monitor as behaviour and fraud patterns change (model drift)
Human oversightHumans review important or disputed decisions
AccountabilityClear responsibility for errors and how they are fixed

Example: an AI flags a payment as fraud; the bank should verify with the customer and allow appeal instead of treating the alert as proof.

18. Consent, transparency and grievance redressal

  • Consent: informed, specific and freely given; not hidden in long fine print. The Account Aggregator system is built on explicit consent.
  • Transparent terms: interest, fees, risks and data use stated clearly (e.g. Key Fact Statement for loans).
  • Accessible complaints process with an acknowledgement or reference number.
  • Timely resolution within set timelines; if unresolved in 30 days, the customer can approach the RBI Ombudsman.
  • No misleading practices in ads or app design.

These build trust and reduce disputes. Example: before accepting a digital loan the customer must see the APR, fees, repayment schedule and penalties, and have a clear route to dispute wrong charges.

Practice questions

Expand and explain the CIA triad with a banking example for each.

Confidentiality (only you see your balance), Integrity (payment amount cannot be changed in transit), Availability (UPI works at peak times).

Give the three factor types in MFA with an example each.

Knowledge (PIN), possession (phone receiving OTP), inherence (fingerprint).

Why don't you need a UPI PIN to receive money?

The PIN authorises debits from your account. Entering it in a 'collect' request or QR scan pays money out; receiving needs no action.

Tokenisation vs encryption: one key difference.

A token has no mathematical link to the original and can only be mapped back through the vault; encrypted data can be decrypted by anyone with the key.

Authentication vs authorisation with an example.

Authentication: logging in with fingerprint (who you are). Authorisation: you can see only your own accounts (what you can do).

Name the three stages of money laundering.

Placement, layering, integration.

Which law and body govern AML reporting in India?

Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002; reports go to FIU-IND.

What is a regulatory sandbox and why is it useful?

A supervised, limited live test of a new financial product with relaxed rules; it lets innovation happen while risks are found before full launch.

State three rules of RBI's digital lending guidelines.

Direct disbursement and repayment between lender and borrower; Key Fact Statement with APR; no access to contacts, gallery or media.

Data privacy vs data security in one line each.

Privacy: should we collect and use this data, and how? Security: how do we protect the data we hold?

List four ethical issues in FinTech.

Privacy misuse, algorithmic bias, lack of transparency, manipulative design (also predatory lending, accountability).

What can a customer do if the bank does not resolve a complaint?

After 30 days (or if unsatisfied), file a complaint under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme at cms.rbi.org.in.

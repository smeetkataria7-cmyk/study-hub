Chapter 1: Introduction to Financial Systems

Q1. Financial System: objectives and importance

A network of financial institutions, markets, instruments and services that moves money from people with surplus funds to those who need funds.

Objectives
Mobilization of savings and allocation of funds to productive uses
Capital formation for expansion and new industries
Providing liquidity (easy conversion of assets to cash)
Risk management (insurance, investment options)
Efficient payment system (UPI, NEFT, RTGS, cards)
Importance
Encourages investment, supports industrial and infrastructure development, creates employment, promotes economic growth and financial inclusion.

Q2. Structure of the Financial System (draw four boxes)

Financial Institutions: collect savings, give loans. E.g. RBI, SBI, HDFC, LIC, NBFCs.
Financial Markets: buy and sell assets. Capital, Money and Derivatives markets.
Financial Instruments: represent ownership or debt. Shares, debentures, bonds, T-bills, CDs, mutual fund units.
Financial Services: banking, insurance, stock broking, digital payments, advisory.

Q10. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

Central bank, established 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act 1934.

Objectives
Price stability (control inflation), adequate credit, financial stability, safe payment system.
Functions
Issue of currency (except one-rupee note and coins)
Banker to the Government
Banker's bank (supervises banks, lender)
Monetary policy (money supply and interest rates)
Foreign exchange management under FEMA
Regulation of banks (licences, monitoring)

Q11. SEBI

Regulator of the securities market. Established 1988, statutory powers under SEBI Act 1992.

Objectives
Protect investors, orderly development of the capital market, fair and transparent trading, prevent fraud and insider trading.
Functions
Regulating stock exchanges (BSE, NSE); registering intermediaries (brokers, merchant bankers, MFs); monitoring IPOs; investor education; inspection and investigation.
Powers
Inspect records; impose penalties; suspend or cancel registration; prohibit insider trading and price manipulation.
Investor protection
Makes rules for fair trading, monitors exchanges, acts against fraud, grievance redressal, investor awareness.

Capital formation: primary and secondary market

Capital formation = savings of investors turn into long-term funds for companies.
Primary market (new shares)
Direct flow of money from investor to company; IPO (private company lists publicly), FPO or rights issue (existing company issues more). Creates new productive capital.
Secondary market (stock exchange, existing shares)
No new cash to the company; provides liquidity; encourages future primary investment; sets fair market price.

Mutual funds

A fund pools money from many investors; a fund manager buys a mix of stocks, bonds etc. Investors own units. Lowers risk.

How it works
Pooling money, buying a basket, getting units, sharing profit and loss, daily price is the NAV (Net Asset Value).

Derivatives: futures and options

F&O let you speculate or hedge on an asset's price without owning it.

Futures: binding agreement to buy or sell at a predetermined price on a future date. Both sides are obligated. E.g. buy at Rs 1,000; price Rs 1,050 gives profit, Rs 950 gives loss.
Options: buyer gets the right, not the obligation, to buy or sell at a set (strike) price by expiry. Buyer pays a premium.
Call option = right to buy (expect price to rise). Put option = right to sell (expect price to fall).

Chapter 2: Banking and Financial Services

Q1. Traditional Banking System

Conventional banking where customers visit a branch for deposits, withdrawals, loans and payments.

Features
Branch-based, face-to-face service, paper-based transactions, fixed banking hours, personal relationship.
Functions
Accept deposits, give loans, payment and fund transfer (cheques, DD, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS), locker facility.
Importance
Safe place for money, supports economic development, builds customer confidence, promotes savings discipline, stability of the financial system.

Q4. Role and functions of banks in the Indian financial system

Banks are intermediaries between savers and borrowers.

Role
Encourage savings, finance industry and agriculture, support infrastructure, promote financial inclusion, enable digital payments, deliver government schemes (DBT).
Functions
Accepting deposits, providing credit, creating credit (lending part of deposits), payment and settlement, agency services, general utility services (lockers, forex, advice).

Q5. Financial Inclusion

Providing affordable, accessible financial services to all sections, especially low-income, rural, women and weaker sections.

Objectives
Services to every citizen at affordable cost; encourage formal savings; improve credit access; promote digital payments; inclusive growth.
Benefits
Better standard of living, more savings, less dependence on moneylenders, supports entrepreneurship, transparent DBT.
Challenges
Limited access in rural areas, low financial literacy, poor internet, low and irregular income, cyber fraud fear.
Initiatives in India
PMJDY (zero-balance account, debit card, insurance, overdraft); Digital India; UPI; DBT; PMMY (Mudra) collateral-free loans.

Q6. Digital Transformation in banking

Use of digital technology for faster, safer, convenient banking.

Changes
Online and mobile banking, digital payments (UPI, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, wallets), 24×7 banking, paperless banking (account opening, KYC), use of AI, cloud, data analytics (also detect fraud).
Advantages
Quick and convenient, saves time, lowers operating cost, better customer satisfaction, promotes financial inclusion.
Challenges
Cybersecurity (hacking, phishing), low digital literacy, poor connectivity, technical failures and downtime.

Q7. Evolution of Electronic Banking (stages)

1. Traditional banking: branch visits, manual paper records.
2. Computerized banking: computers for records and transactions; still branch dependent.
3. ATM banking: Automated Teller Machines for cash withdrawal, balance check.
4. Internet banking: access account, transfer funds, pay bills online.
5. Mobile banking: smartphone apps, anytime anywhere.
6. Digital banking: internet, mobile, UPI, AI, cloud combined; no branch visit needed.

Q11. How banks make money

Net Interest Income (main source): pay depositors low interest (about 2 to 4%), lend at higher rates (about 8 to 12%). The gap is Net Interest Margin (NIM).
Other (non-interest) income
Fees: transaction/account fees, wealth management commissions, locker rent, forex margins.
Treasury operations: interest on government bonds.
Investment banking: IPO, underwriting, M&A advisory fees.

Q13. NPA

Non-Performing Asset: a loan on which the borrower has not paid interest or principal for 90 days (3 months).

Why bad for the economy
Bank must keep provisions from its profit, so profit falls.
Banks lower deposit rates and raise loan rates for honest borrowers to recover losses.
High NPA means the bank runs out of cash to give new loans.

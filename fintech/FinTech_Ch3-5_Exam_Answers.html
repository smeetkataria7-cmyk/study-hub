Chapter 3: FinTech ecosystem

Q1. Explain the evolution of FinTech in India over the last 2–3 decades. (10 marks)

Introduction: India has built one of the world's three largest FinTech ecosystems, driven by government digital infrastructure, smartphone adoption and supportive regulation. Its journey has four phases.

FOUNDATION (pre-2000) → GROWTH (2000–15) → ACCELERATION (2016–20) → CURRENT (2020+)
  1. Foundation (before 2000s): computerisation and Core Banking Solutions; ATMs; ECS; RTGS and NEFT made electronic transfers possible.
  2. Growth (2000–2015): IMPS (2010) for instant 24×7 transfers; Aadhaar and e-KYC enabled paperless onboarding; wallets like Paytm gained traction; PMJDY (2014) opened accounts for the unbanked; digital NBFCs such as Bajaj Finserv widened credit.
  3. Acceleration (2016–2020): UPI launched in 2016; demonetisation pushed digital payments; startups grew in lending, wealth (Zerodha, Groww) and insurance (Policybazaar).
  4. Current (2020–present): COVID-19 accelerated contactless payments and video KYC; Account Aggregator framework enabled consent-based data sharing; embedded finance (Amazon Pay Later with IDFC First); RuPay credit cards on UPI; e-Rupee pilot.

Conclusion: with UPI and India Stack at the centre, India moved from branch-based banking to a mobile-first financial system in about 25 years.

Q4. Explain FinTech business models in brief with examples. (10 marks)

  • Payment gateway (MDR model): Razorpay, Cashfree charge 1–3% per card or wallet transaction.
  • SaaS and hardware: UPI is free, so Paytm and PhonePe earn rent from soundboxes and POS machines.
  • P2P lending: Faircent matches lenders and borrowers; earns processing and management fees.
  • Co-lending / NBFC: Lendingkart scores and sources borrowers; bank funds the loan; earns interest spread or commission.
  • Discount brokerage: Zerodha, Groww charge a flat ₹20 per intraday or F&O order, free delivery.
  • Robo-advisory: smallcase earns subscription or management fees.
  • Insurance aggregator: Policybazaar earns commission per policy sold.
  • Neo-insurance: Acko earns a share of premium on micro-policies.
  • Neo-banking (partner bank): Fi and Jupiter share interchange fees and interest with Federal Bank.

Q5. What are the challenges related to cryptocurrency in India? (5 marks)

Crypto is legal to hold and trade but is not legal tender; it is treated as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA).

  1. Restrictive taxation: flat 30% tax on profits, no set-off of losses, 1% TDS on transfers above ₹10,000 which drains traders' liquidity.
  2. RBI opposition / no legal tender: RBI sees risks to monetary sovereignty and stability and prefers the e-Rupee; no regulator protects investors if exchanges fail.
  3. AML compliance: exchanges must register with FIU-IND under PMLA; unregistered offshore exchanges are blocked.
  4. Strict reporting: detailed disclosures cross-checked with exchange data; penalties push users abroad (capital flight).
  5. Volatility and scams add risk for retail investors.

Q6. Explain how Policybazaar disrupted the insurance sector in India. (10 marks)

Before 2008 insurance was sold through agents who pushed high-commission products, with mis-selling and little transparency. Policybazaar (PB Fintech) changed this through:

  1. Push to pull: a comparison marketplace where customers compare and choose policies themselves.
  2. Price transparency: premiums, riders, exclusions and Claim Settlement Ratios displayed openly, forcing insurers to compete and lowering premiums.
  3. Promoting term and health insurance instead of high-commission ULIPs and endowment plans.
  4. Claims support: PB Care, digital claim assistance, hospital and garage locators.
  5. Asset-light model: takes no insurance risk; earns commissions; scales through online traffic at low marginal cost.

Conclusion: it made insurance customer-centric, cheaper and more transparent.

Q7. What is CRED's business model and how did it acquire customers so successfully? (10 marks)

Business model: a gated platform for users with a credit score of 750+. Bill payment is free; revenue comes from brand partnerships and listing fees, lending (CRED Cash, CRED Mint), transaction fees of about 1–1.5% on RentPay, and insurance and wealth referrals.

Customer acquisition:

  • 750+ gate created an exclusive, aspirational image.
  • Rewarded an existing habit (paying card bills) with CRED coins, cashback and games.
  • Viral, high-production advertising with retro celebrities.
  • Heavy early spending on rewards to build an affluent user base before monetising.

Q8. Why do businesses use Razorpay (paid) when Google Pay / PhonePe are free? (5 marks)

  • More payment methods: 100+ including cards, net banking, EMI, BNPL, international cards; free apps mainly support UPI.
  • Website integration: automatic order confirmation, invoicing and stock updates; with free apps the owner checks payments manually.
  • Subscriptions: UPI AutoPay and card mandates for recurring billing.
  • Smart routing: reroutes payments when a bank is down, raising success rates.
  • Dashboards: refunds, settlements, split payments, reports and fraud tools.

Chapter 4: Digital financial services

Q10. Describe the architecture and working of a mobile banking system. (10 marks)

Client tier (Android / iOS) ──TLS──▶ API Gateway + IAM (OAuth2, MFA, rate limit)
   ──▶ Microservices (core banking integration, payments, accounts, notifications)
   ──▶ Data layer (encrypted SQL/NoSQL)
Cross-cutting: security · compliance (RBI, PCI DSS) · monitoring
  • Client tier: native apps handle UI, biometric/PIN login and encrypt requests.
  • API gateway and IAM: single entry point; validates tokens, MFA, consent, rate limits.
  • Microservices: independent services for core banking, payments, accounts, notifications.
  • Data layer: encrypted databases for records, ledgers and audit trails.
  • Governance: encryption and key management, RBI/PCI DSS compliance, monitoring.

Working: login → encrypted request over HTTPS → gateway validates → payments service and core banking update ledgers → data saved and audited → notification sent and confirmation shown.

Q11. What are digital wallets? Explain their features, applications and working. (10 marks)

A digital wallet is a software application that stores payment credentials and/or prepaid money and lets users pay quickly online and in stores (e.g. Paytm, Amazon Pay, Apple Pay). RBI calls them Prepaid Payment Instruments (closed, semi-closed, open).

Features: stored value or linked accounts; QR, NFC and one-click payments; tokenisation; storage of loyalty cards, tickets and coupons.

Applications: e-commerce checkout, P2P transfers, in-store QR payments, transport and FASTag, bill payments.

Working: initiate (scan/tap) → authenticate (biometric/PIN) → token generated and encrypted → gateway routes to network switch → issuing bank verifies and debits, merchant credited → receipt and alerts.

Q13. Define APIs. Explain their role in financial services and how they enable communication between apps and banks. (10 marks)

An API is a set of rules and protocols that lets one application securely request data or services from another.

Role (open banking): lets FinTechs build on bank infrastructure without exposing core systems; decouples old core banking from new front ends; enables instant KYC, credit checks and balance queries; connects payment networks, e-commerce, bureaus and insurers.

App ─REST/JSON + OAuth token─▶ API Gateway ─validate─▶ translate ─▶ Core Banking
App ◀────────── encrypted JSON response ◀─────────── result
  1. App sends a REST request in JSON for the user.
  2. Gateway validates API key, signature, OAuth 2.0 token, rate limit.
  3. Request translated and routed to the core banking system.
  4. CBS executes and returns the result.
  5. Gateway sends an encrypted JSON response; app updates.

Applications: e-KYC, payment gateways, Account Aggregator, BNPL, credit bureau checks, UPI.

Q14. What are P2P payment systems? Explain the working of Google Pay (UPI) and compare with PayPal. (10 marks)

P2P payment systems let individuals transfer money directly to each other electronically (UPI apps, PayPal, Venmo).

Google Pay (UPI) working: (1) choose contact/scan QR and enter amount; (2) Google Pay as PSP sends an encrypted request to the NPCI switch; (3) user enters UPI PIN, verified by the bank; (4) NPCI instructs payer's bank to debit and payee's bank to credit in real time; (5) notifications and receipt.

Module 5: Risks, security and regulations

Q15. Explain the major cybersecurity challenges and types of financial fraud in digital finance. Suggest prevention techniques. (10 marks)

Challenges: phishing and social engineering, malware and ransomware, identity and credential theft (SIM swap), data breaches, service disruption (DDoS), insider and vendor risk.

Fraud types: phishing (fake site steals login), UPI/QR fraud (victim scans QR 'to receive money' but pays), card fraud (skimming), impersonation (fake customer care asks for OTP), illegal loan apps.

Prevention:

  • Never share PIN, password, CVV or OTP.
  • Use official apps; check URLs.
  • UPI PIN is never needed to receive money.
  • Strong passwords and MFA; transaction alerts.
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi; keep software updated.
  • Report fraud immediately to the bank and helpline 1930.
  • Banks: AI fraud monitoring, device binding, limits for new payees.

Q16. Explain the CIA triad and its importance in FinTech security. (5 marks)

       Confidentiality
          /     \
   Integrity ─── Availability
  • Confidentiality: only authorised access. Example: a customer cannot see another's balance. Controls: encryption, MFA, access control.
  • Integrity: data stays accurate. Example: ₹1,000 cannot become ₹10,000 in transit. Controls: digital signatures, hashing, audit logs.
  • Availability: services up when needed. Example: UPI at peak hours. Controls: backups, redundancy, DDoS protection.

Q18. What is multi-factor authentication? How does it improve security? (5 marks)

MFA verifies a user with two or more different types of factors: knowledge (password, PIN), possession (phone/OTP, token) and inherence (fingerprint, face). Working: password → second factor → access. It improves security because a stolen password alone cannot open the account, reducing account takeover; e.g. UPI needs the registered phone plus UPI PIN.

Q19. What are KYC and AML? Explain the role of RBI in regulating digital financial services. (10 marks)

KYC verifies customer identity (physical, Aadhaar e-KYC, video KYC, CKYC). AML prevents illegal money being made to look legitimate (placement → layering → integration), under PMLA 2002 with reporting to FIU-IND. They stop fake accounts, flag suspicious patterns (mule accounts), and support investigations.

Role of RBI:

  • Regulates and supervises banks, NBFCs and payment systems (PSS Act 2007).
  • Customer protection: limited liability for unauthorised transactions; Integrated Ombudsman Scheme.
  • Cyber-security and IT risk directions.
  • KYC Master Direction and AML rules.
  • Innovation: regulatory sandbox, Account Aggregator, e-Rupee.
  • Digital Lending Guidelines 2022.

Q20. What is the RBI regulatory sandbox? Explain its purpose and working with a diagram. (10 marks)

A regulatory sandbox is a controlled, supervised environment where new FinTech products are tested with limited real users, for a limited time and with some relaxed rules, before a wide launch. RBI's framework (2019) runs in themed cohorts.

Application → Screening → Test design → Assessment → Testing (limited users/time) → Evaluation → Exit / launch
  • Customer protection: risks found early.
  • Controlled testing within limits.
  • Encourages innovation without full licensing burden.
  • Risk assessment of security and compliance.
  • Regulator learns and writes better rules.
  • Product improved before launch.

Example: an AI lending app tested with a few thousand users before full launch.

Q21. Discuss the regulatory challenges and ethical issues faced by FinTech companies. (10 marks)

Regulatory challenges: multiple regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI) for multi-product apps; regulation lagging technology and sudden rule changes; data privacy and localisation (DPDP Act, payment data stored in India); evolving KYC/AML and deepfake risk; Digital Lending Guidelines (direct disbursal, KFS with APR, no contact access); high compliance cost.

Ethical issues: privacy misuse, algorithmic bias, lack of transparency, dark patterns, predatory lending, accountability gaps, digital divide.

Way forward: responsible AI (fairness, explainability, human oversight), clear consent, transparent terms and grievance redressal.

Q22. Explain responsible AI and the importance of consent, transparency and grievance redressal in digital finance. (10 marks)

Responsible AI principles: fairness, transparency/explainability, privacy and security, accuracy and monitoring, human oversight, accountability. Example: an AI fraud alert is verified and appealable, not treated as proof.

  • Consent: informed and specific, not hidden in fine print (Account Aggregator model).
  • Transparency: interest, fees, risks and data use clear (Key Fact Statement).
  • Grievance redressal: easy complaint process, acknowledgement, resolution within timelines; escalation to the RBI Ombudsman after 30 days.
  • No misleading marketing or design.

These build trust and prevent disputes.
